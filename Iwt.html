01 · The question

Turning measurements into an estimate

This supervised-learning project uses physical measurements to estimate fish weight in grams. The goal is to understand how well a straightforward regression model captures the relationship in the available data.

Project objective

Train a model to predict the continuous target Weight from a fish’s category and body measurements, then evaluate its performance on held-out records.

Why regression?

Weight is a numeric quantity. Linear regression offers an interpretable baseline that connects each input feature to a predicted value.

05 · The results

A promising baseline, with room to improve

These are the reported metrics from the notebook’s 48-record test set.

Test-set coefficient of determination
0.840

R² = 0.8398. On this split, the model explains about 84% of the variation in observed fish weights.

How to read the metrics

R² score0.8398 / 1.0
Mean absolute error99.59 g

MAE is the average absolute difference between predicted and actual weight on the test set. It is measured in grams; lower is better.

Important limitation: These results come from one train/test split, not repeated cross-validation. Linear regression can also produce implausible negative estimates and may not capture non-linear growth patterns. Treat this as an educational baseline, not a production or scientific weighing tool.
06 · Next steps

What I would explore next

Stronger validation

Use cross-validation and compare errors across different splits to check how stable the model is.

Better feature handling

Represent species as a categorical variable, investigate correlated length measurements, and compare non-linear models.
